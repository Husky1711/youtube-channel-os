[ON SCREEN: "Education only - not investment advice"]

## BEAT 1 - Pausing in a fall
A SIP buys more units when prices are low. That's the whole point of it. Pause during a fall and
you skip the cheapest units you'll ever get, then restart when they're expensive again.
[ON SCREEN: two rupee bags - same amount, more units in the dip month]

## BEAT 2 - Never stepping it up
Your salary goes up every year. Your SIP doesn't. A step-up of even 10% a year changes the end
number more than picking a "better" fund does.
[ON SCREEN: two lines, flat SIP vs step-up SIP, labelled "illustration, assumed return"]

## BEAT 3 - Checking the wrong number
People check the absolute return. The number that tells you how you're actually doing is XIRR,
because it accounts for when each instalment went in. It's on your statement.
[ON SCREEN: a statement with XIRR circled]

## PAYOFF
So: don't pause in a fall, step up once a year, and read XIRR, not absolute return. The checklist
is in the description.
[ON SCREEN: three-line checklist]
